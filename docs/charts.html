How the chart is ranked
  1. Pool. Every game Steam lists among the top sellers for the category's tags, plus every game on the Addiction page.
  2. Category. A game belongs to a chart when one of its tags (…) is in its own top 5 Steam tags, the ones most players voted for. Games with 4X, RTS, turn-based strategy, incremental, idler or clicker in their top 5 are left out, same rules as the Addiction page.
  3. Score. Average players for the month: Steam's player count taken every hour, averaged over every hour of that calendar month (from SteamCharts). For the few games SteamCharts doesn't track, the site samples Steam's live count every 2 hours itself, and those games are marked sampled. Older months for those games are estimated from a once-a-day count and marked est.
  4. Minimum. At least 50 average players to qualify.
  5. Ties. The higher peak for the month goes first.
  6. Movement. The same chart, built with the same rules, for the month before. ▲ 3 means up three places, NEW means it wasn't in that chart (not out yet, or under the minimum).

Rank = sort by AvgPlayers(month) high→low, then PeakPlayers(month) high→low

    Good to know

    Average players
    How many people were in the game at the same moment, averaged across the month. A game with 5,000 average players has roughly 5,000 people playing at any given time of day.
    Peak
    The highest number of people playing at once during the month, usually a weekend evening or launch day.
    Typical player
    Median hours played by the game's most recent reviewers, from the Addiction page, shown when the game is tracked there.
    Keep in mind
    Only players on Steam count: Game Pass, Epic, console and offline players aren't included. Past charts are rebuilt with today's tags, so a game whose tags changed may move between categories.

    Player counts from SteamCharts, which records Steam's own hourly numbers. Tags from the Steam store. Updated on the 2nd of every month. Source and settings on GitHub.
